C. Reading and language focus 13 min

Subject: Change of POD – Hong Kong instead of Shanghai (Product Y)
Hi Wei,

Following your message, I’d like to confirm that the port of discharge for Product Y would be changed from Shanghai to Hong Kong. Before the booking is updated, I need to have a clear picture of a few points. First, who should be named as consignee on the B/L? Second, could you tell me whether a notify party has to be added? Finally, the delivery address must be given in full, as the forwarder will not be able to issue the documents without it.

Could you also let me know who will arrange the customs clearance and the onward transport? If it is easier, we can have a short call tomorrow to go through everything.

Best regards,
Marie
  1. List the four pieces of information Marie asks for.
  2. Find and write three passive forms used in the email.
  3. Why does the forwarder need the delivery address?
Verbs that are NOT followed by “about”: discuss (the plan, about), mention (the address), contact (someone), answer (a question), request (information). Compare: talk about, speak about, think about (these do take “about”). Also: information about/on the consignee or regarding the consignee.

Correct the errors in the original agenda sentence:

“Can we discuss about the new POD in HK port instead of Shanghai for Neoderm? I need to have a clear view and information regarding the consignee on the B/L and delivery address to be mentionned.”

Indirect questions – more polite than direct questions

Direct: Who is the consignee? → Indirect: Could you tell me who the consignee is? (normal word order – no “is the consignee”, no “do/does”)
For yes/no questions use if / whether: Do you know whether a notify party is required?
Useful openers: Could you tell me… / Do you know… / I’d like to know… / Would you mind letting me know… / Could you confirm…

D. Grammar review – the passive voice 12 min

Form: be (in the right tense) + past participle.
present simple: The containers are booked for 1 December. · past simple: The address was not mentioned. · present perfect: The B/L has been issued. · future: The goods will be delivered next week. · modals: The consignee must be stated. · passive infinitive: …the address to be mentioned.
Use the passive when the doer is unknown or unimportant, when the focus is on the process or document, in formal writing, and to soften bad news (The additional quantity cannot be provided sounds less personal than We cannot give you…).

Exercise 1 – Put the verb in brackets into the passive.

  2. The consignee (must / state) clearly on the B/L.
  3. The B/L (already / issue) by the carrier.
  4. The goods (deliver) to the warehouse next week.
  5. The documents (should / amend) before the vessel arrives.
  6. The delivery address (not / mention) in your previous email.
  7. We (inform) by the carrier that the vessel is delayed.
  8. When the allocation file (send)?

E. Fluency – the clarification call and email 15 min

Task 1 – Live Teams Role-Play: the clarification call (8 min)

Teams Live Task Role-play a live Teams call with your trainer (acting as your distributor) about changing the POD from Shanghai to Hong Kong. Aim for about 3 minutes, following the structure below, then debrief with your trainer and run a smoother second round if time allows:
  1. Open – say why you are calling (I’d like to go through the change of port…)
  2. Ask – at least four indirect questions (consignee, notify party, delivery address, customs clearance / onward transport)
  3. Check – confirm what you understood (So, if I understand correctly, …)
  4. Close – summarise next steps and deadlines (The documents will be sent as soon as…)

Task 2 – Write (7 min)
